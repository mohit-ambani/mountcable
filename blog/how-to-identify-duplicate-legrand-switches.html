How to Identify Duplicate Legrand Switches: Myrius and Arteor Checks

By Mount Cable India · July 21, 2026 · 35 years serving Bengaluru

The higher a switch is priced, the more a counterfeiter earns per fake — which puts Legrand's Myrius and Arteor ranges squarely in the crosshairs. Buyers paying premium rates assume the premium itself guarantees authenticity. It does not. What guarantees authenticity is verification, and a premium switch actually gives you more to verify: better finishing, more precise moulding, more consistent packaging. Every gap between the copy and that standard is evidence.

Where the copies fall short

  • Finish and feel. Genuine Legrand switches have precise moulding, uniform surface texture and a firm, damped click. Duplicates feel lighter, sound hollow, and often vary piece to piece within one box.
  • Plate alignment. Myrius and Arteor plates sit perfectly flush with even shadow gaps. Warped frames, uneven gaps or modules that need force to seat are strong warnings.
  • Embossing on the back. Genuine units carry crisp brand embossing and moulded ratings on the rear. On copies the embossing is shallow, blurred, or the lettering is subtly wrong.
  • Series name. Check that Myrius or Arteor is spelt and styled exactly right on the module, the plate and the carton. Typography errors are a recurring counterfeit tell.
  • Packaging. Sharp print, correct colours, accurate multilingual text. Where Legrand provides a QR code, hologram or scratch-verification feature on the pack, verify it before payment.

The price test is your strongest filter

Premium brands have well-established market prices, and the electrical trade's 3 to 5 percent margins mean no genuine dealer can go far below them. A Legrand quote that looks like a steal is the oldest duplicate signature in the business. Collect two or three quotes; the consensus defines the genuine market, and the dramatic outlier defines the risk.

The channel test

Legrand sells through an authorised network. Ask your seller to show the authorisation certificate, and insist on a GST bill that names Legrand and the series.
