Best Modular Switch Brands in India (2026): Anchor vs Legrand vs Schneider vs Greatwhite

By Mount Cable India · July 22, 2026 · 35 years serving Bengaluru

Modular switches and wiring devices are one of the few electrical decisions homeowners actually see and touch every day, so the brand choice matters as much for look and feel as it does for underlying build quality. Anchor, Legrand, Schneider Electric and Greatwhite are four of the most established names in the Indian market, and each occupies a distinct position worth understanding before you commit a whole home to one brand.

Where each brand generally sits

Anchor by Panasonic is the most familiar switch brand in Indian homes, backed by Panasonic, with a range that spans from entry-level to premium lines — making it a natural default for many buyers. Legrand is a global player in premium wiring devices, known in India through ranges like Myrius and Arteor aimed at buyers who want a design-forward, higher-end finish. Schneider Electric brings global engineering credentials to its Livia and Zencelo ranges, positioned similarly at the premium end. Greatwhite is a newer, design-led Indian brand that has built momentum with modern-looking ranges such as Fiana and Myrah, competing on design and value together.

Match the brand tier to the room

You rarely need to run one brand across an entire home. A common and cost-effective approach is to reserve a premium range for spaces guests actually see and touch — the living room, main bedroom and entrance — and use a value-tier range for utility areas such as store rooms, balconies and back-of-house switch points. This lets you get the visual upgrade where it is noticed most, without paying premium pricing on every single point in the house.

Frequently asked questions

Which modular switch brand is best in India?
For most homes any of the established brands bought genuine will serve well, and the choice comes down to the series rather than the badge. Premium series differ in contact material, switch action, plate finish and module range, all of which matter more than the brand name on the box.
How much should I budget per switch point?
Roughly ₹180 to ₹350 per point for entry ranges, ₹350 to ₹700 for mid ranges, ₹700 to ₹1,500 for premium and ₹1,500 upwards for designer or touch ranges, including the module, its share of the plate and the mounting box.
What is the real difference between a cheap and an expensive switch?
The contact material inside, which decides how many thousand operations the switch survives, and the mechanism, which decides whether the action stays crisp. Plate material, module range and colour consistency across batches also improve with tier.
When do I have to choose the switch brand?
Before wiring, because mounting box sizes and module widths differ between brands and the boxes fitted during the conduit stage commit you to a brand family. The plate colour and finish can be decided much later.
Are duplicate modular switches common?
Yes, especially of premium ranges where the price gap makes copying profitable. Buy sealed boxes from an authorised dealer, check moulding and print quality, and operate the switch several times before accepting it.
