Which Switch Brand Should You Buy? A Budget-Wise Guide to Hifi, Elleys, Lisha, GM, Legrand and Norisys

By Mount Cable India · July 23, 2026 · 35 years serving Bengaluru

Walk into any electrical shop and ask for "a good modular switch" and you will likely be shown five or six different brands, each at a different price point, each with someone confidently calling it the best. The truth is simpler than it sounds: there is no single best switch brand for every home. The right pick depends on how much you want to spend per switch, and — just as importantly — what you personally value most: raw durability, a modern look, or plain value for money. This guide lays out the budget ladder and the priority-based picks so you can make that call for your own home rather than relying on whichever brand the shop in front of you happens to stock.

The budget ladder: what a rough spend per switch buys you

Modular switch pricing in India moves in fairly clear bands. The figures below are illustrative bands to help you orient yourself, not fixed quotes — always confirm current rates for the exact range and finish you want.

Budget per switchSuggested brandWhy
₹8 – ₹12HifiA very economical entry-level option, well suited to utility areas, back rooms and spaces where function matters more than finish.
₹13 – ₹17ElleysA step up in fit and finish while still sitting at a tight budget, a sensible choice when you want more rooms covered without stretching spend.
₹18 – ₹22LishaA solid mid-economy choice with decent variety across plate styles and modules.
₹23 – ₹28GM (GM Modular)Known for a wide range of colours and modern finishes at a reasonable price, popular where look matters as much as function.
₹30 – ₹50LegrandInternational engineering with a reputation for durability and long service life, generally the safer premium pick for main living areas.
₹75 – ₹85NorisysA designer, top-tier segment aimed at statement interiors where the switch plate itself is part of the décor.

These bands are illustrative and can move with the specific range, colour and module count you choose — treat them as a starting point for planning, not a final price.

Or pick by what matters most to you

If the budget ladder still leaves you unsure, it can help to work backwards from your own priority instead:

  • Durability and quality matter most to you. Legrand is generally the recommended pick. It carries international engineering behind it and a long track record for holding up over years of daily use, which is exactly why it is often chosen for main living areas and entry points that see the heaviest daily handling.
  • You want more colours and a fashion-forward look. GM is worth a close look. It is known for offering a wide range of colours and contemporary finishes at a price that stays reasonable, so you can match switch plates to your interior scheme without paying designer-segment prices.
  • You want the best value for money. Lisha, Vinay or Elleys are all reasonable picks here. None claims to be the most premium option, but each delivers workable quality at a price that keeps a whole-home switch budget under control.

Why many homeowners mix brands room by room

One pattern we see constantly at the counter is homeowners deliberately mixing brands rather than picking one for the entire house. This is not indecision — it is usually the more sensible approach. A living room, main entrance or bedroom sees daily use and constant visual attention, so it makes sense to spend more there on a brand like Legrand or a designer range like Norisys. A utility area, store room, or back balcony is used far less and is rarely on display, so an economical brand like Hifi or Elleys does the job just as well at a fraction of the cost. Spreading your budget this way — premium where it is seen and touched most, economical where it is not — usually delivers a better outcome than spending evenly across every room, or overspending on switches nobody notices.
