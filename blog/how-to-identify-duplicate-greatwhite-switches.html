How to Tell Genuine Greatwhite Switches from Duplicates (Fiana and Myrah)

By Mount Cable India · July 21, 2026 · 35 years serving Bengaluru

Popularity has a tax, and counterfeiting is how it gets collected. Greatwhite's Fiana and Myrah ranges have grown fast on the strength of design and finish — and copies have appeared just as fast. The counterfeiter's bet is simple: a switch is chosen by its face, bought by the box, and screwed into a wall where nobody re-examines it. The brand loses its reputation one failed fake at a time, and the buyer lives with arcing contacts behind a pretty plate.

Five checks before the boxes are billed

  • Fit and finish. Genuine Greatwhite switches have smooth, uniform surfaces and assemble without force. Rough moulding edges, flash lines and forced fits are counterfeit tells.
  • Plate alignment. Fit one module into its Fiana or Myrah plate: genuine parts sit flush with even gaps all round. Warped or misaligned plates fail this instantly.
  • Embossing on the back. Turn each sample over. Genuine units have sharp brand embossing and clearly moulded ratings; copies show shallow, blurred or misspelt lettering.
  • Series name printed correctly. Fiana and Myrah should appear with exactly the right spelling and styling on the product and the packaging — typography is where copies slip.
  • Packaging and verification. Crisp print, correct colours, no spelling errors; and where Greatwhite provides a hologram, QR code or scratch-verification feature on the pack, verify it at the counter before paying.

The price test

Keep one number in mind: 3 to 5 percent. That is the margin the electrical trade runs on, which means no genuine seller can price Greatwhite far below the market and survive. A quote that looks like a steal across a full house's switch order is the classic signature of duplicate stock. Take rates from two or three shops; distrust the dramatic outlier.

The channel test

Order through authorised dealers, ask to see the authorisation certificate for the brand, and take a GST bill naming Greatwhite and the series supplied. Fakes travel bill-free by design. The broader method is laid out in original vs duplicate electrical products.

Frequently asked questions

How do I tell a genuine Greatwhite Fiana or Myrah switch from a duplicate?
Look for clean moulding with no flash or misaligned seams, sharp and correctly placed branding, a firm consistent switch action, a module that seats flush in the frame without force, and sealed packaging with good print quality.
Do duplicates copy the packaging as well?
Usually they try, and print quality is where they fall short. Copied artwork loses definition and drifts in colour with each reprint, so logos and small text look slightly soft. Comparing against a box you know is genuine is the quickest check.
Why do counterfeit switches run warm?
Because the contact material inside is under-specified, so resistance at the contact is higher than it should be and rises further as the mechanism wears. A warm switch or socket plate is an early fault signal that should be investigated immediately.
Is it worth matching switches and switchgear from one brand?
It is convenient and keeps warranty responsibility in one place, and several brands including Greatwhite sell both. For switchgear specifically the more important point is that MCBs and the distribution board come from the same family.
How should switches be bought?
In sealed boxes, from an authorised dealer, with an invoice naming the brand, series and module type. Buy them at the end of the build, after painting, so they are not damaged on site.
