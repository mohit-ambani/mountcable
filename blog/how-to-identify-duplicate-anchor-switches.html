How to Identify Duplicate Anchor Switches (Penta and Roma)

By Mount Cable India · July 21, 2026 · 35 years serving Bengaluru

Penta and Roma are arguably the most recognised switch series in India, which makes them the most profitable to counterfeit. A fake switch is even more dangerous than a fake wire in one respect: you touch it every day. Substandard contacts arc, heat and pit; brittle plates crack; and the failure happens at your fingertips. Buyers rarely inspect a switch beyond its face, and counterfeiters rely on exactly that habit. Anchor by Panasonic, to be clear, is the victim here — the copies trade on quality the brand actually delivers.

Hold the switch, then judge it

  • Fit and finish. A genuine Anchor switch feels solid and operates with a clean, positive click. Duplicates often feel light, gritty or spongy in action.
  • Plate alignment. Genuine plates sit flush, with uniform gaps and no warping. Misaligned modules and uneven gaps are a manufacturing tell.
  • Brand embossing on the back. Turn the switch over. Genuine units carry clean, sharp brand embossing and ratings moulded into the body. Shallow, blurry or misspelt embossing is decisive evidence.
  • Series name printed correctly. Penta and Roma should be spelt and styled exactly right on the product and the box. Counterfeiters get typography wrong surprisingly often.
  • Packaging. Crisp print, correct colours, accurate spelling, intact inner trays. Where Anchor provides a hologram or QR-based verification on the pack, use it at the counter.

The price test

Switches are bought in dozens, so a few rupees per unit feels trivial — and that is how fakes get in. Keep the arithmetic in mind: the electrical trade runs on 3 to 5 percent margins, so no genuine seller can go far below market on Anchor. A per-piece price that looks like a steal across a 60-switch order is the classic duplicate signature. Compare rates at two or three shops before deciding.

The channel test

Buy from authorised dealers and distributors, ask to see the authorisation certificate, and insist on a GST bill naming Anchor and the series. Unbilled switch stock is where the counterfeit supply chain lives; our piece on original vs duplicate electrical products explains the wider pattern.

Frequently asked questions

How do I spot a duplicate Anchor Penta or Roma switch?
Check the moulding for flash, seams that do not meet cleanly and slightly soft edges, compare the print quality of the branding on the module, feel the switch action for a crisp defined click rather than a vague one, and confirm the packaging is sealed with sharp printing.
Do duplicate switches actually fail?
Yes, and predictably. The contact material inside decides how many thousand operations a switch survives, and counterfeits economise there because it is invisible. The result is a switch that becomes loose, warm or intermittent within a couple of years.
Is a warm switch plate a sign of a fake?
It is a sign of a poor connection or an undersized contact, both of which are more common in counterfeit modules. A warm switch or socket should be investigated by an electrician regardless of brand, because it is an early warning of a fault.
How can I check before the switches are installed?
Buy in sealed boxes, open one and compare it against a module you know is genuine, look at print sharpness and moulding consistency, and operate the switch several times. Switches are bought at the end of a build, so there is time to check them properly.
