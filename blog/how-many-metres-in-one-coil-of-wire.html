How Many Metres Is One Coil of Wire? 90 m, 180 m, 300 m Explained

By Mount Cable India · November 11, 2026 · 35 years serving Bengaluru

One coil of house wire in India is normally 90 metres. Finolex also sells 45 m, 180 m and long coils of 300 m (1.0 to 2.5 sq mm) or 200 m (4.0 and 6.0 sq mm). Always check the length printed on the carton, because a price quoted 'per coil' means nothing until you know the metres.

How to work out the price per metre

Divide the coil price by the metres in the coil. Longer coils cost less per metre and leave fewer joints. Per-size prices: 1.0, 1.5, 2.5, 4.0, 6.0 sq mm.

Why 90 metres

The 90 m coil is the long-standing trade standard for house wire in India; it replaced the older 100-yard coil, which is about 91 metres. Wire is sold by the coil, not cut to length, because a sealed carton is what carries the batch number, the QR codes and the warranty.

How many coils a house needs

A 2BHK typically needs 3 to 4 coils of 1.0 sq mm, 4 to 6 of 1.5 sq mm, 3 to 5 of 2.5 sq mm and 1 to 2 of 4.0 sq mm, depending on the number of points and the run from the distribution board. Work out your own figure with the wire quantity calculator or read how much wire a 2BHK or 3BHK needs.

Check the length you were sold

Genuine wire has the running length printed along the insulation at every metre, so the first and last markings on a coil tell you its length. A coil sold as 90 m that measures short is a standard trick with loose or re-wound wire. Buy sealed cartons.

Frequently asked questions

How many metres are there in one coil of wire?
One coil of house wire in India is normally 90 metres. Finolex also sells 45 m and 180 m coils, and long coils of 300 m in 1.0 to 2.5 sq mm and 200 m in 4.0 and 6.0 sq mm. The length is printed on the carton.
How many metres is one coil of 2.5 sq mm wire?
The standard coil is 90 metres. In Finolex, 2.5 sq mm is also sold in 180 m and 300 m coils.
How do I calculate the wire price per metre?
Divide the coil price by the number of metres in the coil. Compare brands and coil lengths per metre, not per coil, because a 180 m or 300 m coil costs less per metre than a 90 m coil.
Is a 300 metre coil cheaper than 90 metre coils?
Per metre, yes. A long coil works out cheaper than the same length bought as 90 m coils and leaves fewer joints. It suits a full house or a project; for a small job the 90 m coil avoids leftover wire.
How can I check a coil really has 90 metres?
Genuine wire carries a running metre marking printed along the insulation. Read the marking at the two ends of the coil; the difference is the length. Buy sealed cartons, not loose or re-wound wire.
