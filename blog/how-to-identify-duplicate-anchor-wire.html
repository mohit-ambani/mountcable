Genuine or Fake? Verifying Anchor by Panasonic Wire

By Mount Cable India · July 21, 2026 · 35 years serving Bengaluru

Anchor is one of those rare names that home owners themselves ask for, and that is precisely why counterfeiters print it. When a brand sells on trust built over generations — now backed by Panasonic — a fake label does most of the selling. The counterfeiter's other ally is the product itself: wire is bought by the coil, installed within days, and sealed inside walls where its real quality is invisible until something goes wrong.

Verify the wire, not just the box

The most reliable evidence is printed on the insulation itself. Before billing, have the shop draw out a few metres and check:

  • Brand and size printing — the Anchor name and the conductor size in sq mm, printed cleanly and at even intervals along the wire.
  • ISI / standards marking — present and legible, not smudged or half-printed.
  • Sequential metre marking — numbers that rise steadily. This protects you from fakes and from short coils at the same time.
  • Copper colour — bright and uniform at the cut end; dull grey strands suggest recycled metal.

Then judge the packaging

Counterfeit packaging betrays itself in small ways: slightly fuzzy logo printing, a wrong shade of the brand colour, spelling mistakes in the small text, or a label pasted crooked. Where Anchor provides a hologram, QR code or scratch-based verification on the pack, use it at the counter before you pay — a genuine seller will encourage this, not resist it.

The 3-to-5-percent rule

If you remember one thing from this article, make it this: electrical distribution runs on margins of 3 to 5 percent. A genuine seller mathematically cannot price Anchor wire far below the market. So a quote that feels like a steal is not luck — it is the classic signature of duplicate stock. Take quotes from two or three shops; the consensus is the real price, and the dramatic outlier is the trap.

The channel is the safeguard

Buy from authorised dealers and distributors, ask to see the authorisation certificate for the brand, and take a GST bill that names Anchor and the exact item. That bill is your proof for warranty and, in the worst case, for insurance. Our guide to verifying an authorised dealer shows the questions worth asking — they apply to any brand.
