Reading the Legrand MCB price list

Three things decide the price of a DX3 breaker: the number of poles, the rating band and the curve. Poles are the big step, and that is where most house wiring money goes: a single pole for each outgoing circuit and a double pole or SPN for the incomer. The rating band matters only at 40A and above, so specifying a 32A where a 25A would do costs nothing extra, but specifying a 40A doubles the price. Curve is C for almost every residential circuit.

How many MCBs a house needs

A 2BHK typically needs one DP incomer, one 30 mA RCCB and 8 to 10 single-pole MCBs; a 3BHK 10 to 14. The MCB selector picks the rating for each circuit from its load and wire size, and the MCB, RCCB and RCBO guide explains which device does what.

Genuine DX3 vs copies

Legrand MCBs are copied because the brand carries a premium. A genuine DX3 has the integrated label holder, the biconnect lower terminals and the sliding bottom clamp described in the MPL, and a crisp laser-printed rating label. A soft, unevenly printed label or a breaker that feels light is the first warning. Our guide to identifying a duplicate Legrand MCB goes through it step by step.

What is the difference between C curve and D curve Legrand MCBs?
C curve trips at 5 to 10 times rated current and suits lighting, sockets, ACs and geysers. D curve trips at 10 to 20 times and is for motors, pumps and transformers with high starting current. D curve costs more: ₹678 against ₹494 for a 6 to 32A single pole.
Does Legrand make an MCB that fits a modular switch plate?
Yes, the Myrius modular MCB, 16A or 20A single pole C curve in 1 module, at ₹732 MRP. It is used for AC and geyser points where the breaker sits on the switchboard.
